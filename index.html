Read a year of transcripts at once.

After twelve months you remember session 1, session 9 — the big one — and last week. Thera.py restores the middle: it counts the things in a transcript that are too small to hear one at a time, plots them across the whole course, and makes every number clickable back to the lines that produced it.

Paste a year of sessions, or drop the files

German or English, mixed freely; the interface is in English.

A line like --- Session 7 — 2024-03-14 ---, ### Sitzung 7 or a bare date starts a new session. Without one, a long text is cut into equal segments instead.

Every name you confirm becomes “Person A” before anything is computed. Switch this off if the transcripts are already de-identified, or if the consent and the data protection are settled elsewhere and you would rather read real names.

Drop files anywhere on this panel. .txt · .md · .vtt · .srt · .json (Whisper) · .csv · .tsv · .docx · .html

Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored. Your transcripts are read and analysed inside this tab — switch off your Wi-Fi and everything still runs. Reload and it is gone; whatever you want to keep, you export yourself.

The five views
What was read
What each file actually contained, and which figures are therefore unavailable. It comes first on purpose.
Session
One page per hour: language markers, affect through the session, talk ratio, question types, dropped threads.
Trends
The whole course as trajectories, with changepoints and who enters and leaves the narrative.
Keywords
What is distinctive about one session, which vocabulary is rising or fading, and any word you name plotted across the year.
Concordance
The way back: every number in the tool opens the lines that produced it.
What the numbers can carry

No diagnoses, no scores, no “insights” written in sentences. It counts things and plots them; the interpretation stays your work.

A
Well grounded, robustly computable.
B
Sound reasoning, heuristic implementation. Useful as a trajectory.
C
Exploratory. Interesting to look at, not to conclude from.

And, without softening: one session is noise — below roughly ten sessions most markers mean nothing. A transcript is not a session; tone, pause, body and silence are gone. Speech-recognition errors fall hardest on exactly the rare emotional vocabulary the markers most want to count, and the lexicons are adapted, not validated.

German and English are each read with their own word lists, and nothing is ever translated. Within one language the numbers compare cleanly; across the two they do not, and wherever the tool puts them side by side it says so.

Consent is yours to settle. Your clients consented to being recorded. They almost certainly did not consent to computational analysis of their language — that call is yours to make deliberately, and it is worth raising with your professional body.

Who are these people?

These look like personal names, but only you know which of them are people. Each one you confirm is replaced by a stable placeholder (“Person A”) before anything is computed; the mapping stays in this tab.
