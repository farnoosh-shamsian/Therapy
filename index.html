Read a year of transcripts at once.

After twelve months you remember session 1, session 9 — the big one — and last week. Everything in between has collapsed into an impression. Thera.py restores the middle: it counts the things in a transcript that are too small to hear one at a time, plots them across the whole course, and makes every number clickable back to the lines that produced it.

It runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and you can check that yourself in thirty seconds — see below.

Paste a year of sessions, or drop the files

German or English, mixed freely; the interface is in English.

A line like --- Session 7 — 2024-03-14 ---, ### Sitzung 7 or just a date on its own line starts a new session. Without any such line a long text is cut into equal segments instead, and the report says so.

Drop files anywhere on this panel. .txt · .md · .vtt · .srt · .json (Whisper) · .csv · .tsv · .docx

What you get

What was read
Per file: format, language, speaker labels, how the text was split into sessions — and which figures are therefore unavailable. It comes first on purpose. Whoever sees curves before they learn the speakers were guessed has already believed the curves.
Session
One page per hour: language markers, affect through the session, talk ratio, question types, and threads that were dropped.
Trends
The year at once. Every marker as a trajectory with Bayesian changepoints, and who enters and leaves the narrative.
Keywords
Which words carry the case: what is distinctive about one session against the others, which vocabulary is rising and which is fading, and any word you name plotted across the whole text.
Concordance
Every number in the tool is clickable and opens the lines that produced it. Without the way back this would be a dashboard you watch instead of a person you listen to.

Nothing is uploaded, and you can check that

This page is a set of static files; your transcripts are read into memory and analysed there, by a Python runtime that also runs in the browser. Load the page, switch off your Wi-Fi, and keep working — everything still runs. An application that transmitted your data could not do that. The flip side, so that it is said: results live only in this tab. Reload and you start over. Anything you want to keep, you export yourself.

No diagnoses, no scores, no severity ratings, no “insights” written in sentences. It counts things and plots them. The interpretation is your work and stays your work.

Two languages, two rulers

German and English sessions are each read with their own word lists, and nothing is ever translated — a translated lexicon would measure the translation. Within one language the numbers compare cleanly; across the two they do not, because the word lists are different sizes. The language is detected per file, shown in the report, and correctable in one click; anywhere the tool puts the two side by side, it says so.

Exploratory. Interesting to look at, not to conclude from.

And, without softening: one session is noise — below roughly ten sessions most markers mean nothing. A transcript is not a session; tone, pause, body and silence are gone. Speech-recognition errors are not random, and they fall hardest on exactly the low-frequency emotional vocabulary the granularity measure most wants to count. The lexicons are adapted, not validated.

Consent is yours to settle. Your clients consented to being recorded. They almost certainly did not consent to computational analysis of their language. That is your call to make — but it should be made deliberately rather than by default, and it is worth raising with your professional body.

Who are these people?

Thera.py has guessed at personal names in the text. You know which of these are actually people; the machine does not. Whatever you confirm is replaced by a stable placeholder (“Person A”) before anything else is computed. The mapping from placeholder to name stays in your browser and is never stored.
